## Who I am

I am Kong, a software engineer and technical lead working on smart hardware, IoT, audio/video and SaaS products. I care not only about whether a feature can be built, but also whether it solves a real problem, runs reliably and can be delivered sustainably by a team.

My work spans mobile, backend and systems engineering: from Android and iOS development to Java and Go services, cloud-native infrastructure and engineering collaboration. My responsibilities also include architecture, technology choices, core implementation, planning and code review. I focus on moving product, engineering and teams forward together.

## Engineering problems I care about

I keep returning to questions such as how connected devices reach the cloud reliably, how media services remain stable, and how a product can grow from a feature into a maintainable platform. These problems cross application, service, network, storage and deployment boundaries, requiring explicit trade-offs between architecture, delivery speed and long-term maintenance.

I believe architectural decisions deserve careful thought, and implementations should remain as simple as the problem allows. Design has to stand up to runtime behavior, tests and reproducible evidence—not just diagrams or assumptions.

## Open-source projects

- [SaaS-Zero](https://github.com/saas-zero/saas-zero): a Go microservices foundation for multi-tenant SaaS, exploring tenant isolation, RBAC and platform engineering.
- [mini-ruoyi](https://github.com/Kun-GitHub/mini-ruoyi): a lightweight Go admin system for low-spec servers, focused on resource efficiency, simple deployment and zero CGO.
- [RuoYi-Go](https://github.com/Kun-GitHub/RuoYi-Go): an earlier monolithic experiment in DDD and hexagonal architecture. It is paused and remains available as an architectural reference.

Each project addresses a different context: RuoYi-Go records monolithic layering, SaaS-Zero targets multi-tenant platforms, and mini-ruoyi focuses on low-resource deployment. I state their status and purpose plainly rather than presenting paused work as active development.

## About this site

This site brings together products, open-source work and engineering notes, including the trade-offs behind design decisions and lessons from implementation. More than chasing a particular technology, I care about breaking down complex problems and building software that is reliable, maintainable and useful to the people who depend on it.
